I · Executive Summary

The Quiet Emergence of a National Nutrition Benefit

Over the past four years, Food is Medicine interventions have moved from philanthropic pilots to Medicaid-reimbursed services in roughly one-third of U.S. states — reshaping the fiscal relationship between diet-sensitive chronic disease and public insurance. This briefing maps the full 50-state landscape as of April 2026.

Key Finding

As of early 2026, 16 states have secured CMS approval for Section 1115 demonstrations that reimburse Medically Tailored Meals (MTM) or medically supportive food and nutrition services. An additional cohort of states has implemented meal benefits through narrower authorities — 1915(c) Home and Community-Based Services waivers or Managed Care In Lieu of Services (ILOS) flexibility — placing a total of approximately 22–25 states somewhere on the active-reimbursement continuum.

Policy Inflection

The federal policy environment shifted markedly in March 2025, when CMS rescinded the Biden-era Health-Related Social Needs (HRSN) framework guidance. Existing approvals remain in force, but new HRSN/SDOH requests are now evaluated case-by-case. The 2025 reconciliation law's Medicaid work requirements (effective January 1, 2027) add further uncertainty for pending waiver renewals.

II · Tier Classification

Three-Tier Classification of State Medicaid Food Benefits

States were classified using two variables: (1) the breadth of the legal authority supporting meal reimbursement, and (2) the scope of clinically eligible populations. The resulting taxonomy separates demonstration-scale programs from narrow HCBS carve-outs and states with no formal meal reimbursement.

I
Leaders
Section 1115 HRSN / Food is Medicine Demonstrations

States with active, CMS-approved Section 1115 demonstrations that explicitly authorize Medically Tailored Meals, medically supportive groceries, or produce prescriptions as covered Medicaid services. These states operate at demonstration scale with dedicated fee schedules, statewide infrastructure investments, and independent evaluation frameworks.

California Massachusetts New York Oregon Washington North Carolina New Jersey New Mexico Arizona Delaware Rhode Island Hawaii Nevada Maine Illinois Colorado Pennsylvania
II
Emerging
1915(c) HCBS Waivers · 1915(i) SPAs · Managed Care ILOS

States providing meal reimbursement through narrower authorities — most commonly 1915(c) Home and Community-Based Services waivers for elderly and physically disabled populations, or Managed Care Organization (MCO) In Lieu of Services (ILOS) flexibility. Coverage is typically restricted by age, functional status, or MCO discretion rather than clinical trigger.

Connecticut Maryland Minnesota Virginia Tennessee Oklahoma Michigan Ohio Texas Florida Georgia South Carolina Louisiana Vermont New Hampshire
III
Limited · None
No formal meal-benefit reimbursement under Medicaid

States with no standing Medicaid authority to reimburse for home-delivered or medically tailored meals beyond federal minimums. Nutrition support in these states typically relies on Older Americans Act Title III-C funding, SNAP/WIC, or philanthropic sources rather than Medicaid.

Alabama Alaska Arkansas Idaho Indiana Iowa Kansas Kentucky Mississippi Missouri Montana Nebraska North Dakota South Dakota Utah West Virginia Wisconsin Wyoming

IV · Regional Analysis

Geography as Destiny: Regional Patterns in Food Benefit Adoption

Medicaid Food is Medicine adoption displays strong regional clustering. The Northeast and West Coast dominate Tier I, while the Southeast and Mountain West account for the overwhelming majority of Tier III jurisdictions. This distribution correlates closely with three variables: Medicaid expansion status, MCO penetration, and Governor-level health equity policy priorities.

Northeast
9 states

The region's Tier I density is unmatched. Massachusetts pioneered the Flexible Services model in 2020; New York, New Jersey, Maine, Rhode Island, Delaware, and Pennsylvania followed with 1115 approvals. Connecticut, New Hampshire, and Vermont operate via narrower HCBS/ILOS pathways.

West
13 states

California's CalAIM Community Supports framework (2022) is the nation's most mature. Oregon, Washington, Arizona, Nevada, New Mexico, Hawaii, and Colorado maintain 1115 food benefits. Utah, Idaho, Montana, Wyoming, and Alaska remain Tier III.

A two-track region. Illinois is the sole Midwestern Tier I state. Michigan, Ohio, and Minnesota operate meaningful HCBS/ILOS meal programs. The balance — Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Missouri, Nebraska, the Dakotas, Wisconsin — provide no formal Medicaid meal benefit.

Three Observed Regional Drivers

Medicaid expansion correlation. All 17 Tier I states adopted ACA Medicaid expansion; 14 of 18 Tier III states are either non-expansion or late-expansion jurisdictions. Expansion states carry lower uncompensated-care costs, making downstream hospitalization-reduction savings more directly capturable by state Medicaid agencies.

Managed care penetration. States with MCO penetration exceeding 75 percent of Medicaid enrollment adopt ILOS-based food benefits at roughly triple the rate of fee-for-service-dominant states. California, Ohio, and Texas exemplify the ILOS pathway; Alabama, Wyoming, and Alaska — predominantly fee-for-service — lack the contractual vehicle.

Gubernatorial sponsorship. Seven of the most recent Tier I approvals (Illinois, Colorado, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Hawaii, Nevada, Maine) correlate with direct Governor-level advocacy. The National Governors Association's 2025 Food is Medicine initiative (Governors Healey of MA, Stitt of OK, Polis of CO, Hochul of NY) has become the dominant cross-state coordination vehicle.

Key Policy Risks to Pipeline States

Budget conditionality. North Carolina's Healthy Opportunities Pilots — the most rigorously evaluated state program in the country — faces discontinuity as the 2025–2027 biennial budget negotiations did not include ongoing operational funding. A program credited with 23 percent hospitalization reductions may lapse on the margin.

Federal alignment pressure. The January 1, 2027 deadline for Medicaid work requirements in the ACA expansion population diverts state agency bandwidth from HRSN implementation. Several states have paused or withdrawn pending 1115 filings to conserve administrative capacity.

Evaluation compression. Most Tier I states are in year two or three of five-year demonstration cycles. The evidentiary case for renewal requires rigorous outcomes data — hospitalization rates, A1C trajectories, cost-offset estimates — on timelines that may precede sufficient follow-up duration.

VI · Methodology

Data Sources & Analytic Approach

State classifications were derived by cross-referencing CMS-posted Section 1115 demonstration approvals, state Medicaid fee schedules, managed care contract filings, and independent policy tracker data. Reimbursement rates reflect the most recently published fee schedules as of April 2026; where states employ capitated ILOS payment structures, the rate shown represents the predominant fee-for-service equivalent.

Tier assignments are interpretive: a state may operate partial benefits under multiple authorities. Where a state appears in multiple categories (e.g., California has both a Section 1115 demonstration and extensive ILOS activity), the higher tier classification is applied. Pipeline status is based on CMS filings current through Q1 2026.
